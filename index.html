Sound familiar?

Plenty of AI options. Not much clarity on what comes next.

Every leadership team has been told AI will change how they work. Most have tried a pilot or two, and most already pay for platforms with AI built in. The harder part is turning all of that into results the business can count on. Whatever your industry, it usually comes down to four things.

Almost everyone is using AI. Far fewer are seeing it pay off.

Card 1 · Prioritising

“Where do we start?” So many processes could use AI, and every one has a champion. Without a clear way to rank them, the most valuable work waits while the loudest request moves first. The cost: effort spreads thin. Gartner expected at least 30% of generative AI projects to be abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025, with unclear business value among the reasons.

Card 2 · Scaling

“The pilot worked. Now what?” A promising demo is a long way from something hundreds of people rely on. Security, integrations and ownership all need answers before it can grow. The cost: good ideas stall. The average company scraps 46% of its AI proofs of concept before they reach production.

Card 3 · Time to value

“When will we see a return?” Long programmes ask for patience before anyone sees a number. Meanwhile the hours and the money keep slipping. The cost: momentum fades. 60% of senior leaders outside the C-suite expect scaling barriers to take a year or more to clear.

Card 4 · Aligning with business outcomes

“Is this moving what matters?” AI goals and business goals drift apart easily. Activity gets measured, results don’t, and the next step gets harder to justify. The cost: spend without a story. Half of CEOs say the pace of AI investment has left them with disconnected technology, and only 39% of organisations see any EBIT impact from AI.

If any of these sound like your week, you’re in good company. They’re the reason Simpliigence AI exists.

Every quarter a process stays manual, it costs you twice.

Once in hours and margin, and again in the good people who spend their week on work a well-built agent could handle. Closing that gap doesn’t take a big programme. It takes one process, done properly, with a number attached. That’s where we start.
